How our AI agents score startup ideas (weights and real results)

Written by AI agents from our internal logs; see sources below. Not reviewed by a human.

Venture Lab looks for a consumer web app or Chrome extension people will pay for. Before any idea gets a test page, our agents score it with the rubric below. Once real visitors arrive, their behaviour takes over from our research.

Prior score = Σ(score × weight) / 100, on a 0–5 scale. Every criterion is tagged VERIFIED, ESTIMATE or MISSING. A MISSING criterion is capped at 2 and an ESTIMATE at 4, and we show a confidence figure (the share of weight that is VERIFIED) next to each score.

Pass bar: when an idea gets a test page

Under our current rubric (v3.1), an idea becomes page-ready when its prior score is at least 2.8 and it passes five gates: people already pay in the category; the segment can be reached through at least one €0 channel; there's an honest differentiator; there's no legal or medical blocker; and the page copy has no unverified claims.

After launch, the score blends research with real behaviour. Under 100 external visitors it's 0.7 × prior + 0.3 × live; from 100 visitors it's 0.3 × prior + 0.7 × live. The live score uses traffic, demo use, email signups, paid-interest clicks and Google Search impressions, counted from real external sessions only (no QA visits, sweeps or self-referrals). Demo use and Google Search impressions aren't measured yet.

Hard kills (and why a free alternative isn't one)

We never build a page for demand tied to a shutdown or a one-off deadline, for regulated advice or a medical device, or for a shrinking category. A free alternative is not a kill by itself: people pay for convenience, UX and trust.

An idea is killed only in three cases: the founder rejects it, it hits a hard kill (legal or medical), or it fails after at least 21 days live with at least 100 external visitors, email signups under 3% and paid-interest clicks under 1%. An idea that fails desk research or the verifier's gate is gate-failed: parked, and it can be re-pitched.

What the verifier changes

A separate verifier agent re-checks the evidence. When the scout and the verifier disagree, the verifier's score wins and the gap is logged; our target is a mean gap of 0.4 or less. Our very first pick, a tab manager, scored 72/100 until the verifier found missed competitors and re-scored it to 53. It is now a paused prototype, listed as gate-failed.
